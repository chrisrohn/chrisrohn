A daily new-music feed

Every morning the build reads Last.fm, ListenBrainz, MusicBrainz, Bandcamp, Deezer, radio playlists and a few dozen blogs, scores what it finds against one listener's taste, and lists the best of it here. Press play on any card, or space, and it plays on through the list.

Sign in with Google and the Keep / Skip buttons appear: Keep files a track into a year playlist in your own YouTube library, Skip hides it for good. Install the site as an app for a full-screen deck with lock-screen controls.

Cleanup

How this works

The daily build reads every year playlist and lists each song it holds more than once — the same video twice, the same song in two years, two uploads of it (the audio track and the video, say), or two editions of it (the original and a remix, the radio edit and the extended mix). Every copy is laid out with its edition, length, album, position and whether it plays here; ▶ plays a copy in place so two can be heard back to back, and look up the copies asks YouTube what each upload is (1 unit per 50). Choose a copy with its radio button and keep the chosen copy removes the rest; ✕ removes one; move to… refiles a copy into another year (the verified year is marked ✓, and a song filed entirely in the wrong year gets a one-tap move everything); looks fine · dismiss keeps every copy and stops listing the song. A removal costs 51 YouTube API units, a move 102. What you do here is remembered on every device you sign into, and the next build drops what is gone.

Not streamable here lists the tracks that will not play in the US: what the build's scan found greyed out on YouTube Music, plus what the playability audit below finds — deleted, private and region-blocked videos, which YouTube Music's listing hides altogether. The build searches for a playable upload of each (free, overnight) and a swap adds it and removes the dead copy; find a replacement now does the search from here for 100 units.

Scan a year now

The report above is from the last build; a live scan reads one playlist as it is right now (1 unit per 50 tracks), groups it by song the same way, and catches today's double-taps and second uploads before the nightly build does. Removals here need no lookup: the scan already knows each row.

Playability audit

Which tracks cannot play in the US at all: the playlist is read through the YouTube Data API and every video is checked with YouTube (about 2 units per 50 tracks) — a video YouTube no longer answers for is deleted or private, one restricted away from the US is blocked. Findings go under not streamable here above, are kept on this device, and travel to the build with your ratings so it can look for a playable upload of each overnight.

Concerts near Detroit

How this works

Every afternoon the Concerts workflow takes every artist played on Indie Discotheque in the last year — the Last.fm 12-month chart of tt_discotheque, plus anyone filed into this year's playlist — asks Bandsintown where each of them plays next (a batch a day, so the list fills in over its first few runs), reads every music event Ticketmaster lists in the radius when a key is set, keeps what is within 150 miles of Detroit, and looks up each act's most popular song on Last.fm (Deezer without a key) and on YouTube Music, so ▶ plays it right here. The ticket button names the seller where the link says so (Ticketmaster, TicketWeb, AXS, Etix, DICE…) and shows the price where Ticketmaster lists one (Bandsintown publishes none); a Bandsintown link leads on to whichever seller the venue uses. Search matches artists, venues, cities and song titles; space plays the first song on the list, j/k walk down it, and an artist's name opens their sheet.

    Stats

    API activity

    Every request this browser makes to the YouTube Data API, as it happens. The site only ever does three things with it: read a playlist to verify what it already holds, add the track you approved with ▲︎ Keep, and remove one you took back (Undo, restore) or that the Cleanup tab found twice. Nothing runs in the background and nothing writes without a tap. Kept on this device only.

    Settings

    Signing in uses YouTube API Services; see the Privacy Policy and Terms. Anyone can listen here. Sign in with Google and the Keep / Skip buttons appear: ▲︎ Keep adds the track to a year playlist in your own YouTube library right away (curator: <year> | Indie Discotheque; guests: <year> Picks from chrisrohn.com), with Undo for a few seconds. ▼︎ Skip hides it for good. ≠ Wrong video is for a card whose YouTube match plays something other than the title it shows: it hides the card without judging the song, costs nothing, and tells the daily build to look for another upload — the card is back the moment one is found. Your ratings are mirrored to a hidden app-data file in your own Google Drive, so every device you sign into stays in step. Nothing else is touched.

    Google sign-in lasts an hour at a time and renews itself the next time you tap a thumb. Sign out only forgets this device; to disconnect the site from your Google account entirely, remove it at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
